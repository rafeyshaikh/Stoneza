Anantay Exports Pvt. Ltd. · Bhilwara, Rajasthan · since 1992

India cuts the finest stone on earth.
It deserves a brand.

Thirty-four years quarrying and trading it. Own mines at Bijolia, Kota and Asind, and direct access to the best quarries in every stone belt in the country.

The stone is extraordinary. The way it is sold is not. Unnamed, ungraded, uncalibrated, and substituted the moment a drawing leaves the architect’s desk.

Stoneza is a stone company built like a brand — named products, published data, and a code that survives the tender.

Bijolia, Bhilwara district — where it started in 1992
01

The problem was never the stone

India produces some of the finest dimensional stone in the world. Kota limestone that outlasts the building it is laid in. Rajasthani sandstone in colours no factory can imitate. Deccan basalt under half a percent absorption.

Everything between the quarry and the floor is where it goes wrong. Stone is sold here under whatever name the seller prefers, arrives uncalibrated, is graded by whoever loads the truck — and is substituted freely, because if a stone has no name and no code, nothing on paper says it cannot be swapped.

02

What a brand actually fixes

A brand is not a logo on a crate. It is a promise that the thing inside is the same every time.

So we named every product, gave each a spec code that can be written into a BOQ, published the technical data rather than sending it on request, and stated on the product page itself the applications each stone is wrong for. None of that is normal in this industry. All of it is ordinary in every other one.

03

We go where the stone is

India’s best stones are not in one place. Kota limestone under Rajasthan, basalt in the Deccan, limestone beneath Jaisalmer, granite in the south.

So we built for reach. We mine at Bijolia, Kota and Asind, and have spent thirty years earning direct access to the best quarries in every other belt — buying at a scale that puts us first in line for the good lots. Every lot then comes through our own works for grading, calibration and finishing against one standard. Not one hillside — the pick of a country.

If a stone has no name, it has no defence.

What we are building

Three things we are betting on

01

Specification, not supply

The first natural stone brand in India written into drawings by name. When an architect specifies STZ-SF-COBL rather than “black sandstone,” the substitution stops.

Collections, not colours

Twelve named collections — Stonefield, Rawscape, CobbleCraft, Cascade, Stone Glow. Each a way of working with stone, so a designer chooses a language first and a colour second.

India’s stone, worldwide

Indian stone already sits in buildings across four continents — usually unnamed, usually through three intermediaries. We are building the brand that lets it travel under its own name.

What we hold ourselves to

Four promises, plainly

01

Specify the right stone for the job

Our reach across the country means the stone on your drawing is the one the job needs, not the nearest thing we hold.

02

Calibrate, do not just cut

Most stone sold in India is uncalibrated. Ours is machined to one band — the difference between a floor that lasts fifty years and one that drums in five.

Grade before crating, never after

SPEC CONFLICT — resolve before anything ships

The brief and the product sheet disagree on thickness fieldstone_banner_2.pdf states "Fixed 20 mm thickness — every piece is the same thickness" and makes it USP number one.
Stoneza_Product_Data_Sheet_FINAL.xlsx states 18–28 mm for all seven Fieldstone products, and 20–28 mm for White Mint.

These cannot both be true, and it is the single most quoted number on the product. This page follows the brief and uses a fixed 20 mm throughout, because that is the customer-facing claim already in circulation. If the sheet is right, the brief is making a promise the product does not keep — and an architect who measures a delivery will find out. Correct one of the two before this page or any further product page goes live.
Two smaller conflicts in the same brief Coverage: the brief says billed 220 sq ft, lays up to ~240 sq ft. The sheet says 220 sq ft per crate with no mention of the extra. The brief's version is the better story and is now on the page — add the 240 figure to the sheet.

Email: the brief shows sales.support@stoneza.in. Everything else uses sales@stoneza.in. Pick one. Two addresses in circulation splits enquiries and looks careless on a spec sheet.

Weight: 54 kg/m² came from the sheet at 18–28 mm. At a fixed 20 mm the true figure will differ. Flagged on the page as CONFIRM — a facade consultant needs it accurate.

Changes in this revision

  • Removed the coursing-and-joints section and the fixing-method-by-wall-height section, along with every 3 m mechanical-fixing reference elsewhere on the page.
  • Added "Why Fieldstone" — the five USPs from the brief, with hand-blended colour set as the hero panel since the brief names it as the edge. A sixth was added for the 220/240 coverage margin, which the brief treats as a benefit but had not been stated as one.
  • Added "How Fieldstone is installed" — six steps with a site note against each, since this is the question customers ask most. It states plainly that Stoneza does not install, and sets out exactly what the guidance covers.
  • Thickness changed to a fixed 20 mm across the page, glance strip, sizes table, FAQ and schema, per the brief.
  • Colourways reframed as hand-blended palettes rather than seven single-stone colours. This reconciles the brief's blending story with the seven-product range: each palette is its own blend.
  • Four FAQs added from the brief: where the colour variation comes from, coverage per pack, whether it holds up in heavy rain, and whether Stoneza installs. 15 FAQs total, all in schema.

Structure differences from the Cobblestone page

  • No per-variety comparison table. All seven palettes share an identical specification, so seven identical rows would read as padding to a technical buyer. Replaced by one shared spec table plus a colour selector.
  • Installation replaces laying patterns as the on-site section. On cobblestone the buyer's question is which pattern; on cladding it is how it goes on the wall.
  • Corner units get their own cost driver and two FAQs. Sold per running foot, routinely forgotten at estimating stage, and the commonest source of a mismatched wall.
  • "What are the disadvantages of stone cladding?" is answered honestly. It is a live PAA question, it earns trust with a specifying architect, and a straight answer is far likelier to be cited in an AI Overview than marketing copy.
